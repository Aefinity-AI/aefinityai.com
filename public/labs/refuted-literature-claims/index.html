Question
Do published recipes for training small ternary models work as claimed on ordinary hardware?
Why it matters
Recipes repeated in papers and blog posts can fail on ordinary hardware.
How the test works
  1. Record each cited recipe as refuted, corrected or surviving.
  2. Run it on our target machine where we can.
  3. Where we cannot, say so and cite the stated requirement.
What we found
2 recipe claims refuted. A stock Hugging Face recipe for training soft prompts on the Falcon-E model gave outputs wrong by about 300 times on one real layer, as measured by us. The claim that a stock library runs on a free T4 GPU in 2 to 5 hours is marked refuted on a documented hardware requirement; we did not run it on a T4.
What it means
Several widely repeated recipe claims failed when checked: one by measurement, one from a stated requirement. It is our environment and one set of library versions, not proof they fail everywhere.

01Why it matters

Recipes repeated in papers and blog posts can fail on ordinary hardware.

02Method, step by step

  1. Record each cited recipe as refuted, corrected or surviving.
  2. Run it on our target box where we can.
  3. Where we cannot, say so and cite the requirement.

Method as recorded

Our research notes record each cited recipe as refuted, corrected or surviving. The soft-prompt recipe was refuted by execution on our own target machine. The T4 claim was refuted by reading the requirements stated in a cited source, and we did not run it on a T4. Run by us.

03Results, exact and sourced

  1. R1A stock Hugging Face transformers recipe (AutoBitLinear, online_quant=False) for training soft prompts on the Falcon-E checkpoint gave outputs wrong by about 300 times on one real layer (layer-0 q_proj), as measured by us on our target box.

  2. R2The claim that stock onebitllms runs on a free Kaggle/Colab T4 in 2-5 hours is marked REFUTED in our notes. The basis is a documented hardware requirement (Triton >=3.3 needs compute capability 8.0+, and the T4 is sm_75). We did not run it on a T4. The notes say the path may survive only in an unverified older-library environment.

04What it does NOT show

That the recipes fail everywhere. This is our environment and one set of library versions.

05Scope and caveats

  • Our environment and one set of library versions.
  • Entries are our own research notes made public.
  • Run by us; no unaffiliated reproduction.
  • The soft-prompt result is one measurement on one layer of one checkpoint (Falcon-E). The source says the recipe was salvaged in weaker form and requires a step-0 logit-parity gate.
  • The T4 refutation rests on the documented requirement in a cited source, not on our own run. The source says the path survives only behind an UNVERIFIED torch<=2.6 / triton-3.2 / pinned-TRL environment.
  • The source notes there is no independent replication of the soft-prompt path anywhere.

06Outcome

Negative

The claim or approach failed, or we retracted it.

07What we learned

Several widely repeated recipe claims failed when we checked them, so we keep a public list of what failed. For each claim, the list says whether we ran it ourselves or checked it against a source's stated requirements.
