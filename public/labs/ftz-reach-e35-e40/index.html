Question
Does one obscure rule about tiny numbers ever change a real AI output, and how sure can we be?
Why it matters
One obscure rule about tiny numbers is pinned in the spec. If it never matters, is it needed?
How the test works
  1. Count how often tiny values arise (E35, E38).
  2. Analyse exp exhaustively over all 2^32 inputs (E39).
  3. Classify every reduction and normalisation intermediate (E40).
What we found
0 tiny values in 562 billion. Of 562,531,070,920 intermediates classified across both pinned models, none was subnormal (a very tiny number); 0 of 628,547,776 weight values were. On the normative vector the rule does not affect the digest. Two of our earlier explanations for why were wrong and were withdrawn.
What it means
No case was found where the rule changes the digest, but that holds for these two models and prompts only: on constructed inputs the rule can change bits.

01Why it matters

One obscure rule about tiny numbers is pinned in the spec. If it never matters, is it needed?

02Method, step by step

  1. Count how often tiny values arise (E35, E38).
  2. Analyse exp exhaustively over 2^32 inputs (E39).
  3. Classify every reduction and RMSNorm intermediate (E40).

Method as recorded

Counting instruments on six prompts (E35); 20 cells on both models including non-ASCII prompts and 256-token decodes (E38); exhaustive analysis of exp over 2^32 inputs (E39); then classification of every reduction and RMSNorm intermediate (E40). Run by us; corrections recorded as errata E-10 and E-12.

03Results, exact and sourced

  1. R1E35: zero of 21,248,280 exp evaluations reached the band it studied (a band later shown not to be where the flush rule acts).

  2. R2E38's wider sweep found 2 arguments in that band on one long decode (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 256 tokens); the digest did not move.

  3. R3E39: the mechanism we had given in four places was wrong; the conclusions survive, the reason did not.

  4. R4The claim that a denormal arises in a real decode was withdrawn.

  5. R5E40 classified 562,531,070,920 intermediates across both pinned checkpoints; none was subnormal.

  6. R6No weight operand in either model is subnormal.

  7. R7Conclusion: on the normative vector the rule does not affect the digest.

04What it does NOT show

That the rule is unnecessary in general: on constructed inputs it can change bits in the softmax division step. The finding holds for the two pinned checkpoints and the stated prompts only.

05Scope and caveats

  • Read the four documents in order; two of our own mechanism claims were retracted, while the measurements stood.
  • Holds for the two pinned checkpoints and the stated prompts only.
  • Run by us; no unaffiliated reproduction.
  • E39 shows the flush rule can change bits in the softmax division step on constructed inputs; the finding is only that it did not on the normative vector.
  • E40 reports a defect in E39's own counting instrument, found by a control and fixed; the source states it could not bite at that call site.
  • The 2-argument E38 finding was in a band that E39 later showed is independent of the flush rule.

06Outcome

Null

No effect found, or nothing yet to report.

07What we learned

We never found a case where the flush rule changes the digest. The explanations we first gave for why were wrong, and we published the corrections next to the exhaustive count that replaced them.
