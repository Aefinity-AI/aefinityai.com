The same picture, in words

Question
Were our speed measurements measuring our code, or a hidden hardware throttle?
Why it matters
A speed number is meaningless if the machine was secretly throttled.
How the test works
  1. Print the CPU's current speed ratio against the requested ratio at boot.
  2. Apply a fix: clear the hardware throttle flag and request the maximum speed state.
  3. Generate the same 214 tokens with two same-day builds.
What we found
Running at 5 of 27 speed. The CPU was clamped to core ratio 5 of a requested 27 while the temperature sensor read cool. Every performance number from that machine before 2026-07-29 was taken at about a fifth of nominal clock, and we retracted them. After the fix the ratio went from 5 to 25 on 3 of 3 cold boots. We do not report a speedup.
What it means
Measure the machine before measuring the code. One laptop and three boots; the two halves of the fix were not tested separately, and whether it survives suspend is untested.
Everyday comparison
Like timing a runner who is secretly wearing ankle weights: the time says nothing about their training.

01Why it matters

A speed number is meaningless if the machine was secretly throttled.

02Method, step by step

  1. Print the current core ratio against the requested ratio at boot.
  2. Apply a fix, clearing bd-PROCHOT and requesting the maximum P-state.
  3. Generate the same 214 tokens with two same-day builds.

Method as recorded

A boot-time diagnostic printed the current core ratio against the requested ratio on a Dell Inspiron 15 (i5-5200U, bare metal, no OS). Two builds made the same day, differing only by the STAGE 7 fix (clear bd-PROCHOT and request max P-state), generated the same 214 tokens with a 14.17M-parameter model. Run by us.

03Results, exact and sourced

  1. R1The CPU was clamped to ratio 5 of a requested 27, with PROCHOT asserted while the thermal sensor read cool.

  2. R2Every earlier number from that box (before 2026-07-29) was taken at roughly a fifth of nominal clock, and was retracted.

  3. R3After the fix, cur_ratio went from 5 to 25 on 3 of 3 cold boots (one machine).

  4. R4Wall-time ratio across the two same-day builds was 3.861x, below the 5.000x the ratio change implies; output was byte-identical. Not reported as a speedup.

  5. R5Published as a retraction.

  6. R6Limitation of the before/after: no clear-only arm.

04What it does NOT show

A speed-up, durability across suspend, or the effect of each half of the fix, since there was no clear-only arm.

05Scope and caveats

  • One machine (n=1 laptop, 3 cold boots); run by us; no unaffiliated reproduction.
  • The fix (MSR 0x1FC bit 0) is about fifteen years old; the post cites prior art and does not claim it as new. The measurement error is the point.
  • The fix stage changed two things at once (cleared bd-PROCHOT and requested the maximum P-state); no clear-only arm was run, so the ordering hypothesis is untested.
  • Durability is untested: unknown whether the clear survives S3 suspend or an EC re-assert after a firmware event.
  • The 3.861x vs 5.000x gap is described as a crude indicator of memory-bound work, not a measured decomposition.
  • The post's tok/s column is too coarse to quote; tick counts are the instrument.

06Outcome

Negative

The claim or approach failed, or we retracted it.

07What we learned

Measure the machine before measuring the code. We retracted every earlier number from that box instead of reporting a speed-up.
