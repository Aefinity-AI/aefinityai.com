What we have learned

Twelve lessons, each tied to its labs.

Each lesson cites the labs it comes from. All are from our own runs, at the scopes stated in those labs.

  1. 01

    Lesson 1: Check the computation, not just the text it produced.

    Identical printed losses hid different checkpoint bytes. An altered RMSNorm order changed the digest and left the 16 tokens alone. Edited embedding rows hid from output checks that no prompt exercised.

    From the labs

    • L06One flipped bit in the weights: which checks notice?, Positive
    • L07Realistic edits to the embedding table, including one hidden row, Positive
    • L15E25: the RMSNorm multiply order matters, and our spec sentence saying otherwise was false, Positive
    • L09Ordinary logging versus replay-based receipts on one-field edits, Positive
    • L12Training checkpoints across CPUs and instruction sets, Mixed
  2. 02

    Lesson 2: In our fp32 tests, the pinned order of operations determined the bits.

    The same order matched across the CPUs and the one GPU we tried, and a different order broke the digest; default library kernels did not match across two CPUs in training. Heavily vectorised builds matched when the order was preserved. Scope: one small model, a few machines, run by us.

    From the labs

    • L03Batching and precision on a Tesla T4: which shortcuts change the digest, Positive
    • L19E29: does the compiler's optimiser change the bits?, Positive
    • L01CIS-2: the same AI output, bit for bit, on different machines, Positive
    • L12Training checkpoints across CPUs and instruction sets, Mixed
  3. 03

    Lesson 3: A spot check can give a comfortable wrong answer.

    4,096 samples per binade never hit the worst points among about 8.4 million. A maximum measured on one prompt was only that prompt's maximum, and widening the sweep corrected our own published number.

    From the labs

    • L16E26: sin/cos at very long contexts — the sampled answer was wrong, Mixed
    • L18E28, E36, E37: every internal activation compared against an independent implementation, Positive
    • L17E27: exp, ln and SiLU on every float32 input, and a defect in our spec, Mixed
  4. 04

    Lesson 4: Trying to break our own work finds the holes.

    A mismatch exposed a spec error, a mis-filed receipt exposed a verifier hole, and exhaustive tests exposed a defect in a spec guard. Each was recorded in the open.

    From the labs

    • L04Clean-room reimplementations: one mismatch (from a memo), one match (from spec v0.1), one partial that exposed a spec error, Mixed
    • L08Receipt-verified agent evaluation, and the hole we found in our own verifier, Mixed
    • L14E24: the spec never says which Unicode version defines a letter, Mixed
    • L17E27: exp, ln and SiLU on every float32 input, and a defect in our spec, Mixed
    • L21E41: the spec pins how text becomes tokens, but not how tokens become text, Negative
    • L13E22: which spec rules does the test vector actually exercise?, Mixed
  5. 05

    Lesson 5: Measure the machine and the shipped binary before measuring the code.

    A throttled CPU and a compiler setting each invalidated speed numbers while everything appeared to work; a soft-float build explained one separate slowness and is one contributing cause in the operating-system comparison, which stays open.

    From the labs

    • L27Retraction: our benchmark laptop was running at about one fifth speed, Negative
    • L28A compiler setting silently deleted our vector instructions, Negative
    • L29What does an operating system cost? A pre-registered result we held back, Held back
    • L30The '16% slower' lookup-table kernel claim, retracted, Negative
  6. 06

    Lesson 6: A number without a measurement behind it is not a number.

    Printed benchmark lines were fixed strings, a source-comment figure did not reproduce, and recipes repeated in the literature failed when we ran them. This is where our rule of provenance on every number comes from.

    From the labs

    • L31Audit of our own removed code: benchmark lines that measured nothing, Negative
    • L30The '16% slower' lookup-table kernel claim, retracted, Negative
    • L33Claims from the literature that failed when we ran them, Negative
  7. 07

    Lesson 7: Our explanations were wrong more often than our measurements.

    A mechanism we gave in four places was wrong while the counts stood, and one of our own null results was a vacuous edit. We now publish the correction next to the measurement.

    From the labs

    • L20E35 to E40: does the flush-to-zero rule ever matter? (after two wrong explanations), Null
    • L13E22: which spec rules does the test vector actually exercise?, Mixed
    • L19E29: does the compiler's optimiser change the bits?, Positive
  8. 08

    Lesson 8: A convenience feature can hide the failure a check exists to find.

    The self-check's automatic re-download would likely mask the corruption a monitor is meant to report. This was inferred, not run on corrupted weights.

    From the labs

    • L11Continuous validation: does a one-byte corruption get caught, and what hides it?, Mixed
  9. 09

    Lesson 9: Integrity has a cost; report it next to the claim.

    2.51 times the plain decode on one old laptop, which the source calls an upper bound, from single runs.

    From the labs

    • L10What receipts cost on a real agent episode, Mixed
  10. 10

    Lesson 10: Passing a test does not mean every rule is tested.

    12 of 16 single-rule breaks were caught. The tie-break and the denormal-flush pin are protected only by inputs the vector never contains.

    From the labs

    • L13E22: which spec rules does the test vector actually exercise?, Mixed
    • L20E35 to E40: does the flush-to-zero rule ever matter? (after two wrong explanations), Null
  11. 11

    Lesson 11: A bar set in advance keeps us honest, and we say when it was not set in advance.

    A 2 ULP bar was missed by the first fix and we kept the bar. A surprising result was held back by a rule we wrote in advance. The final sin/cos mechanism was added after a miss and was not itself pre-registered.

    From the labs

    • L05Sin/cos accuracy for RoPE against a pre-registered 2 ULP bar (v0.2, v0.3, v0.3b), Mixed
    • L29What does an operating system cost? A pre-registered result we held back, Held back
    • L03Batching and precision on a Tesla T4: which shortcuts change the digest, Positive
  12. 12

    Lesson 12: Everything so far has been run by us.

    No unaffiliated party has reproduced the results. Until one does, every claim on this site is 'reproduced by us'.

    From the labs

    • L24Open challenge: the Hall of Divergence, Null
    • L01CIS-2: the same AI output, bit for bit, on different machines, Positive
